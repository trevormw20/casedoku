# Casedoku Manor — Fiction Framing & Improvement Plan

## Core rule
Casedoku Manor is real Sudoku with suspect markers instead of numbers. A suspect appears multiple times on the board, like the number 7 in Sudoku. The board must NOT be framed as a literal map of where each suspect physically stood. It is Inspector Graves' abstract Deduction Grid — used to sort testimony, evidence, contradictions, and suspect relationships. The mystery is carried by clues, evidence, culprit, weapon, motive, proof, final accusation, case-closed explanation, victim memorial, manor restoration, and the Inspector Graves throughline. The Sudoku board is the satisfying logic engine underneath. The player completes Graves' abstract deduction grid to close a cold case.

## Direction: Path A-plus — Own the split
- Sudoku is the comfortable logic engine; suspect markers replace numbers.
- Clues and objectives create the detective layer.
- Final accusation makes the mystery concrete.
- Manor restoration gives emotional progression.
- Player understands: "The grid is not the crime scene. It is the detective's method."
- Success: a new player says "It's Sudoku with suspects, and the mystery gives me a reason to care."

## Guardrails
- Do NOT redesign into Murdoku. Do NOT remove the Sudoku core. Do NOT switch to single-occupancy placement unless playtesting proves the current system fails.

## Preferred vocabulary
suspect marker, deduction grid, case grid, Graves' deduction method, complete the grid, place the marker, follow the evidence, solve the case pattern, close the case, restore the truth.

## Avoid
"where the suspect was", "where everyone stood", "who was in the room", "the suspect was in the Library", "reconstruct the crime scene", "place the suspect where they were".

## Key per-section notes
- Onboarding (P1): teach in 60s that (a) it's Sudoku with suspect markers (each appears once per row/column/room) and (b) the grid is Graves' method, not a crime scene. Title "Graves' Deduction Grid"; Wembly line "the grid is not the crime scene itself; it is how Inspector Graves organized the truth"; short, skippable, cozy noir.
- Clue cards (P3): concise clues like "the Captain is directly above the Widow" are ALLOWED because onboarding establishes markers-not-bodies. Add a small persistent label/tooltip: "Clues refer to Graves' deduction grid." Don't make every clue verbose.
- Case Closed (P8): connect completed grid → accusation → culprit/weapon/motive/proof → case closed → seal → restoration. The completed grid is the method; the accusation is the concrete payoff.
- Restoration (P9): reward for deduction, not proof the board was literal. Loop: complete grid → close case → restore truth → restore manor.
- Demo first 10 min (P12): preempt "why do the same suspects repeat?" → "they're markers in Graves' deduction grid, like Sudoku numbers."
- Playtest (P15): one case per player, 5–10 new players, answer whether the mystery is load-bearing.
- Tone: cozy noir, not robotic; story ~6th–8th grade, gameplay clues ~5th–7th grade.
